Applied Cryptography Lab

The Applied Cryptography Lab focuses on designing practical cryptographic protocols and systems that balance efficiency, security, and privacy in real-world applications. Current emphasis includes threshold cryptography for decentralized and dynamic settings, privacy-preserving authentication, secure multiparty computation, and countermeasures against cryptographic misuse. The work bridges theoretical foundations with deployable solutions for blockchain, financial systems, anonymous credentials, and beyond.

Research Themes

  • Threshold cryptography (e.g., threshold ECDSA/EdDSA/BBS+, best-of-both-worlds designs for dishonest-majority and disruption resilience)
  • Privacy-preserving authentication and processing of encrypted/authenticated data
  • Secure multiparty computation with improved round/communication efficiency
  • Cryptanalysis and secure design of lattice-based schemes and covert channels
  • Anonymous tag systems, rate-limiting nullifiers, and double authentication preventing signatures
  • Applications to blockchain, central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), and witness encryption

Ongoing & Recent Projects

  • Best-of-Both-Worlds Threshold Signatures & MPC (2023–present)
    Developing robust, efficient threshold protocols (e.g., ECDSA, BBS+) that support any threshold, resist disruptions, and feature linear communication via novel threshold linearly homomorphic encryption. Adopted in follow-up works by independent groups.
  • Dual-Regev Covert Channel Analysis (with Hoover Yin)
    Exploring (in)security of lattice-based covert channels; presented at IH&MMSec 2025.
  • Rate-Limiting Nullifiers & Anonymous Tag Systems (ongoing)
    Designing lightweight anonymous rate-limiting mechanisms for blockchains without heavy ZK-SNARKs, with formal security definitions and dynamic membership support.
  • Double Authentication Preventing Signatures (ongoing)
    Fine-grained leakage for conflicting authentications in multi-key identity systems, with hierarchical/wildcard extensions for certificate authorities.
  • Policy-Based Tracing for CBDCs (future direction)
    Balancing privacy and mandated oversight using selective zero-knowledge revelation of transaction policies.

Join Us

The Applied Cryptography Lab is open to motivated individuals who are enthusiastic about bridging theoretical cryptography with practical, real-world impact. Passion for the field — whether through designing protocols, proving security, implementing prototypes, or exploring new applications — is the most important quality we look for.

We welcome candidates with backgrounds in cryptography, mathematics, computational complexity, or related areas. Proficiency in any of these is valuable:

  • Strong mathematical foundations (e.g., algebra, number theory, probability)
  • Computer science skills (e.g., algorithms, distributed systems, security theory)
  • Programming experience for cryptographic prototypes (e.g., Rust, Go, Python, or similar)

You do not need to excel in all three — being good in one or more, combined with genuine enthusiasm and willingness to learn, is sufficient. We especially encourage applications from those interested in threshold cryptography, privacy-preserving systems, lattice-based schemes, blockchain security, or related topics aligned with the lab's focus.

Opportunities include:

  • PhD or MPhil supervision (if applying through NYCU CS programs)
  • Visiting scholar/student collaborations
  • Undergraduate final-year projects or internships

If this sounds like you, please reach out via email to harrywong0223@hotmail.com with:

  • Your CV/resume
  • A short note on your research interests and why you'd like to work in the lab (even 1–2 paragraphs is fine)
  • Any relevant coursework, projects, code samples, or publications (optional but helpful)
